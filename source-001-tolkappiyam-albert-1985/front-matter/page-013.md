If you are a graduate of an Indian University you will find it difficult to make this distinction between Grammar and grammar. I do not find this distinction is usefully made in our class rooms. But one must try to understand how Transformational Generative grammar made a distinction between the grammar of a language and the usages in a language. Frank Palmer discusses a useful distinction between research procedures and grammatical descriptions as one of the marks of Generative Grammar. The Structuralist started with the data and worked upwards and “this requirement was completely unjustified.”⁵

You will see now how the clear distinction between Grammar and usage at the linguistic level helps the distinction between Theory and Data accounting in research.

```text
Grammar  ----------------  Research Theory
   |            Accounting          |
Usages   ----------------  Data
```

The earliest of the commentators ilampūraṇar has had the ingenuity to see such a distinction though he does not seem to realise fully its complications. It will be an interesting investigation to see how far he was original in this attempt and what made him make wrong applications of this principle. This commentator made a distinction between verses that are Basic and Verses that are Operational.⁶ Most probably the two words he used | karuvi | and | vinai | did not allow him to make the proper usage though he might have taken this idea from other related language grammars.
