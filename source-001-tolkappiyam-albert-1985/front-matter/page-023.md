The problem of gaps is very crucial in translating verses of the sort we get in Tolkāppiyam. In the verse form the Grammarian says things in a succinct way, really girded up in words. Here is an example. For a helpful understanding of verse 18 one has to fill in the gaps with the words: in the formation of a vowel-consonant after the line:

> The vowel appears after the consonant.

But filling in all the gaps too freely will leave the translation a free rendering and not a translation. This is only an example. One can attempt to study the merits and demerits of any translation or see the point of balance between a translation and an adaptation by studying the gap stories. I do not intend to make this attempt here. I merely make a proposal.

In all such places where I resisted the danger of sliding into an adaptation from a neat translation the short title gives some help as it is amply illustrated in this particular example in verse 18. Besides this, these short titles help one to locate particular verses and to make easy criss-cross references. Since these titles are not a part of the original text a very free rendering is made keeping in view these two needs. The idea of continuity which I have explained in the notes will also serve to minimize the danger of misunderstanding the text arising from gap problems.
