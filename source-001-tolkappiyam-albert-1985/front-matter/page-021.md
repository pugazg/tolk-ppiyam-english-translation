I shall illustrate this now. If you read verse numbered 5 you will find the following four syllables no, pho-, three, and meas—are stressed among the seven syllables in the line: No phoneme has three measures.

Thus the time taken between ‘no’ and ‘pho-’ with no syllable inbetween, the time taken between ‘pho-’ and ‘three’ with two syllables inbetween and the time between ‘three’ and ‘meas’—with no syllable inbetween is the same. This illustrates what is known as the regular beat system. English prose has a regular beat and rhythm. Added to this we have the falling intonation in this sentence the nucleus being ‘meas’. Let us not forget the fact that in the history of English prosody the idea of vers libre is to be governed by this quality of this language. This will show what important aspect of the language we are now discussing.

Whether a sonorous high sounding poetic piece of old in Tamil should be rendered only in such sonorous grand style in English or should it keep to what is accepted as the language of poetry at a particular period, say, today is a debatable point. But this does not arise here because Tolkāppiyam does not keep a high sounding poetic language of intricate imagery and a maze of sounds. This understanding of the textuality in Tolkāppiyam is very important. I feel commentators have gone a little away from the intentions of the original when they made an attempt to forget this question of textuality and tried to make explications as though the Grammar is a difficult text of poetry.

So, the principle adopted here is to represent the rhythm of the target language that is English discarding the rhythm of the original language Tamil.

I must hasten to add a third quality in my translation to these syllabic and total quality which are natural to English. Since grammar conventions are actually declared in the original as in, for example Latin grammar rule 1, Caesero Orator, a certain tone of a declarative speech is kept in the translation wherever it is necessary and is done without any violence to the normal pattern of English sentences.
