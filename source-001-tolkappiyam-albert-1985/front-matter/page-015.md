## III. The Letter: Phonemic Representation

Tolkāppiyam uses the Tamil word | eḻuttu | at the very first and this can be replaced by the word letter in English. Letter actually, represented in the English tradition too a name, a symbol and a sound value. Quid littera vocatur utrem set recta linea curua an motunda pronuntiatio, said Remigius of Auxerre of 10th Century.⁷ Donatue wrote in his _Ars Grammatica_ “Accidunt uni cuique litterae tria: nomen figura potestas.”⁸

In Tamil too, the word | eḻuttu | meant all the three, a name (nomen), a symbol (figure) and a sound value (potestas). Happily this word etymologically names the act of production of sound as well as the act of committing to writing. Scholars had to rediscover the first sense for the Tamils of today when writing seems to be a thing of sophistication as distinctive from speech. For the English too a letter is taken to mean now a grapheme, a letter on the alphabet.

There was an attempt to show that | eḻuttu | meant only phoneme. This extreme is also wrong; it is an influence from the structuralists who are analysts and researchers who would not find it useful to talk of sound units, writing units and meaning units at the same time. But the author of Tolkāppiyam is a system builder in the lines of Transformalists; he does not use isolation methods or structural analysis as much as he did system findings. The phonetic nature of the Tamil language is such that he did not hesitate to keep the three units together in his study and therefore the word | eḻuttu | meant all the three. Of course he never hesitates to look at things in isolation when ever it is found necessary and therefore the word phoneme is freely used in this translation in other places. He never keeps an exaggerated view of the so called virtue of isolating the units. Tamil scholars who found that the phonemic sense of the word | eḻuttu | has been almost lost, rightly bring this sense back; but it is found, they are caught in the web of the structuralist-Analysists and insist that only the sound unit is meant here.
