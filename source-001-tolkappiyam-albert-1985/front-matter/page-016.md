## IV. Phonetic Vs Phonemic

The use of the phrase phonemic representation in preference to phonetic representation requires justification and explanation.

There are four areas in which the two words phonetic and phonemic come into sharp contrast. The four areas can be called Etymology, History, Transliteration and Comparative Linguistics.

**Etymology:** The word phonetic means belonging to the phone, sound and phonemic means belonging to the phoneme. Since the sound units or family of sounds with a significance on the phonology of Tamil are represented by these writing symbols these are called phonemic rather than phonetic.

**History:** If you look at the semantic change the word phonetics underwent you will find that phonetics from the Greek for sound dates to 1797. This was used to denote the writing symbol of sounds in a contrast to picture representations and in 1850 Phonetics meant the study of sound changes in speech vis-a-vis spelling. Of course if you took this into consideration the phrase phonetic representation is more appropriate for the present purpose. But this history must take into consideration the employement of the word phonemic which has come into wider use meaning belonging in more recent history. Thus phonetic will not represent all history.
