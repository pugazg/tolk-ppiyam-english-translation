This may distinguish the plosive pairs; but it does not distinguish the reminder. “Whilst there may be a voicing difference between | p | and | b | etc., voice may be lacking in both. This leaves the energy difference fortis lenis constant. Auditorily this corresponds to strong versus weak sounds.”¹³ O’ Connor says “there will be always a difference between them on an energy basis.”¹⁴ One can now be sure of the usefulness of the terms Tolkāppiyam uses. Explorations do not stop there. O’ Connor says that the fortis consonants are typically longer in all contexts than the lenis.¹⁵ One likes to examine first of all whether the lenis fortis opposition is not found in the short long vowel opposition, secondly we can examine the basis of the length of the three dependent sounds, thirdly the reference to the rare shorter | m | (Verse 13) which is a soft sound may have also this basis and lastly one can investigate whether the consonant clusters referred to in verse 22 to 30 can lend themselves to any pattern of alignment on this basis. These are merely suggested here because what we try to arrive at is not an answer but an understanding that when we use this energy criterion we have a surer basis for the study of the phonemes on the Tamil phonology where chronemes, short long opposition play a very important role and thereby accept that the notions of hard soft and medial are very meaningful. There is no need whatever, to be apologetic about them as some Tamil scholars are.
