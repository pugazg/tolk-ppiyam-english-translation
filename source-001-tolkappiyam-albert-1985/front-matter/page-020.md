# 3. A Translation into Tamil

Concepts in Translation Theory have developed very fast and therefore there is a need for explaining the basic concepts that were kept in mind when this translation was made. Since a detailed discussion of these concepts will help only the critics of this translation and not an English user of this great Tamil Grammar, only those ideas which are useful to this English knowing student of Tamil are discussed here.

The verse form with its particular rhythm which this work employs is not rendered into English since this is not necessary. There are several reason for this. Any attempt to keep the Tamil verse rhythm in English has resulted in breaking the normal accepted sentence formation in English. Earlier translations show this at several places. The famous ‘dislocation of language’ to make words work harder which started with the English poet Hopkins has no place here for emulation in this translation of a grammar, though commentators have done that unconsciously. Neither Tolkāppiyam makes any such attempt. It versifies and it is not poetry in the strict sense of the word in Tamil. So, the translation is rendered in prose.

But we must take care to remember that the ordinary, common English prose keeps a regular rhythm because English is a stressed syllable language. It is a regular beat language. In the words of J. D. O’ Connor, “The basic rule of English rhythm is that the stressed syllables follow each other at regular intervals of time, that is to say there is the same amount of time between each pair of stressed syllables in a given sentence.”¹ This means that in the translation I have discarded the verse rhythm of the Original Language Tamil and employed the rhythm of the Target Language English.
