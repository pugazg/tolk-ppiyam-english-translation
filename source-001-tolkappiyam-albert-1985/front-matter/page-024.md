# 4. The User and the Critic

There are four persons involved in any translation. They are the author of the original language text, the translator, the user of the translation or the text in the target language and the critic of the translation. Between the author of the original language and the user of the target language stand the translator and his critic with knowledge of both the languages.

These pages are meant directly only for the user of this translation. I make this clear quite often in the following pages. I intend to bring out a volume where I may give all my experiences as a translator and this could be ready only after I had finished translating all the three parts of Tolkāppiyam. I should also talk about the various approaches a scholar should take in studying this great grammar. Only very dim indications of these are given here.

This user must understand this as a Grammar. Certainly a good part of it gives the usages in language. But he should not try to use this exclusively to learn to speak and write Tamil. For this he will get only a limited help. This is a grammar of the Tamil of several centuries past. You can investigate it. Linguistic and Research theories, and its systems discover very useful concepts and make comparative studies. For such scholarly studies Tolkāppiyam is a veritable treasure house. All the great and noble things in the areas of Historical Linguistics, Sociolinguistics, Psycholinguistics, Linguistic Philosophy and Linguistic Anthropology can be brought out in a careful study of Tolkāppiyam. This translation along with all these indications is the first step in this direction. These pages introduce this task to the English student and scholar.
