# Notes

## 7

The phrase Subjective case is used here because the verse insists on the functional aspect of the noun. The Tamil word used in the verse shows this clearly.

## 8

The English reader would do well to see that the idea of function in opposition to morphology and placement value as in the three terms predicate, verb, verbal or subject, noun, nominal respectively is complete in this Tamil Grammar. So predication here does not just mean the use of a verb, a word in this morphological form. Predicate in its equivalent in Tamil `| payaṉilai |` denotes as used in this verse the unit which carries the function. So predication is not necessarily made by a verb a particular morphological form. This difference is very important. The notion of Np + Vp can be usefully applied here.

## 9

Any study of the theory of morphology followed by this grammarian should take into consideration the method used here and elsewhere. It goes bythe research methodology of sampling as it is very evident here in this verse.

## 10

Some commentators think that the Tamil phrase which gives the idea' Whatever be the actual word, means that this case marker can replace any other case marker or it means that this is the most commonly used case market.

This translation is preferred as the Grammarian tries his best throughout to keep a distinction between a semantic unit and a syntactic category. This market as a syntactic category carries the semantic value of receiving whatever it means in other usages.
