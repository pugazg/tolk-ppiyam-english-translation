# 2. A Chapter On The Cases

## Number of cases

### 63

There are seven cases.

## The vocative

### 64

With the inclusion of the vocative, the form of address, it is eight.

## The eight

### 65

They are the noun, `| ai |`, `| oṭu |`, `| ku |`, `| iṉ |`, `| atu |`, `| kaṇ |` and finally the vocative.

## The nominative

### 66

Among them, the bare noun appears in the subjective⁷ case.

## Predication

### 67

There are six ways in which this noun is predicated: pointing out the existence of the thing, expressing a wish or an order, making a statement of the action, forming a question, expressing a quality and taking another name in apposition⁸.

## Compounds

### 68

There are noun compounds and they are also predicated in the same manner.

## Statement of the noun

### 69

It is said that in this position it is better that every noun is explicitly mentioned.
