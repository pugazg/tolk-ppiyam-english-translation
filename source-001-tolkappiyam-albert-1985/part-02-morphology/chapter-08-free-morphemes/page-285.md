# Notes

## 79

Tamil scholars have been using the word morpheme for the Tamil word `| iṭaiccol |` and semanteme for `| uriccol |`. Ilakkuvaṉār in his “Studies in Tholkāppiyam”, Tholkāppiyam (in English) with Critical Studies (Madurai : Kuṟaḷ Neṟi Publishing House, 1963) justifies the word morpheme as the equivalent to `| iṭaiccol |` saying that the definition of `| iṭaiccol |` in verse 247. ‘Corresponds with that given by Vendryes of morphemes’. (Vendryes, J. Language (London : Routledge and Kegan Paul Ltd., 1939).

Some have been using the word Clities for `| iṭaiccol |` and Dr. D. Andiappan makes a distinction between free clitics and bound clitics in his book referred to in the following note. Tamil scholars have been balancing a word from the area of Semantics with a word from the area of Structural Grammar. In using free morpheme and bound morpheme for `| uriccol |` and `| iṭaiccol |` we use structural words which are decided by their semantic content. Thus we are balancing two words of equal content.

Tolkāppiyam says at the very beginning of the chapter on the nouns in verse 155 that all words (morphemes) convey meaning. The Grammarian recognizes the fact that bound morphemes too convey meaning. In verse 452 in the final chapter he says that all bound morphemes are modifiers. These two verses will justify sufficiently our translation equivalents.

These two verses along with verse 295 where the free morpheme is described will easily tempt us to go into a whole set of research analysis to decide how far this Grammar is made on the principle of Semantics and how far it is on Structuralism. We know that the morpheme as the minimum unit of meaning carries the concept of both the areas. We also know that this concept is beset with many contradicting theories. It is a question of what makes the fundamental unit of grammar and semantics or to put it in another way
