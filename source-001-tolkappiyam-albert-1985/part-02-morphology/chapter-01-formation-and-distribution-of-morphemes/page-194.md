## 6

This verse has been explained differently by different commentators. These lines lend themselves to the following translation too:

> When a certain thing is asked for and if  
> it is not available one must state what  
> is available using the phrase ‘nothing  
> other than’.

I find more support for the first kind of translation than for this second type among the Tamil scholars with whom I discussed the verse.

The verse is not a mere statement of a social custom with the merchant community. If this were so it will have no place in the section on the distribution of morphemes. Since the second translation directly indicates the usage of the phrase ‘nothing but’, some commentators are led to believe that this is preferrable. But we must hold that the first translation also belongs strictly to the area of syntactical structure. Having said already that the formation of an answer is to follow the structure of the question and where it can legitimately change, here the grammarian means how the structure of the answer can deviate from the structure of the question in specific cases. The first translation need not make us think that the strict linguistic area is lost sight of. The following verse will also show that the area is still and rightly the distribution of structural elements.
