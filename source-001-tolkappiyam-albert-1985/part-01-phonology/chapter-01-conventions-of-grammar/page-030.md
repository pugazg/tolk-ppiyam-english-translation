# 1. Conventions Of Grammar¹

## The thirty independent sounds.

### 1

From | a | to | ṉ | there are thirty phonemic representations.² This excludes the three occurrences whose realizations depend upon the other sounds.

## The three dependent sounds.

### 2

These occurrences are the shorter³ | i | the shorter | u | and the one with three dots,⁴ and these are similar⁵ to the phonemic representations.

## Length: the five short ones.

### 3

The five phonemes | a |, | i |, | u |, | e | and | o | have each one measure and they are called short phonemes.

## —; the seven long ones.

### 4

The seven sounds | ā |, | ī |, | ū |, | ē |, | ai |, | ō | and | au | take two measures each and they are called long phonemes.

## —: three measure.

### 5

No phoneme has three measures.

## —; further elongation.⁶

### 6

If lengthening becomes necessary, it is suggested by the scholars that one more representation of the required sound is added.

## Quantity of the measure.

### 7

A minute observer finds that one measure is the time taken for a wink of the eye or a snap between the fingers.
