# 9. A Chapter on the Changes in Usage with the Shorter | u |

## Six

### 458

In the word | āṟu | the initial may keep its length.

## Nine

### 459

The phoneme at the final position in | oṉpatu | will not change but will take the empty morph | iṉ |.

## Hundred

### 460

The same rules hold good when | nūṟu | follows.

## Three

### 461

The consonant in | mūṉṟu | is replaced by | n |.

## Four, Five

### 462

The consonants in | nāṉku | and | aintu | do not change.

## Changes in Nine

### 463

The initial sound in | oṉ | takes the change indicated earlier. In the place of the following consonant there appears a double | ḷ |. In the word | nūṟu | it is said that the consonant | n | drops and the | ū | becomes | ā |; | i | and | r | appear in between. The final sound is dropped and there the consonant | m | appears.

## + thousand

### 464

When the word | āyiram | follows, the names of the first two numbers lose their | u |.

## —

### 465

It is not an error to keep the initial sounds lengthened.

## —

### 466

The consonant in | mūṉṟu | is replaced by | v |.

## —

### 467

The consonant in | nāṉku | is replaced by | l |.
