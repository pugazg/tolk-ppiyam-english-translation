# Notes

## 17 — continued

Compare this with the process in English when “get” becomes “getting” or when “hope” becomes “hoped” and ‘hop’ becomes “hopped”. Notice carefully this doubling is not presented in pronunciation. In Tamil the doubling or recurring process is complete as it is found in the pronunciation of the word too. You must always remember this difference. It will be very useful if you read 23rd Chapter “Morphopbonemics and Sandhi” of Robert A. Hall Jr., _Introductory Linguistics_ (New York : Chilton Books, 1964). You will appreciate Tolkāppiyam better if you carefully read these seven pages.

## 18

Some of the verses will give a considerable amount of difficulty if you are not careful to see how they have been made with a sense of continuity from verse to verse by the Grammarian. Here is an example. This verse says: If the word | kuṟai | follows. This means if it follows words of measures weights and numbers. See 165. The word “occurs” in 166 is true to the original; it also means “follows”. So you must see carefully the sense of continuity in a group of verses. Then the verse will be clear. The English determiners, conjunctions and connectives are carefully used in this translation to show this continuity wherever it is possible and wherever the use of it will not leave the translation a free rendering. So it becomes important to see where a repetition of a phrase in your reading from a previous one, as it is required here, will help the understanding of a subsequent verse or verses and also where the sense of continuity ends. The short titles given here will also help the reader.

Verses 165 to 168 for example, form a unit with continuity.
