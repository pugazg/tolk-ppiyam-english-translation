# 5. Conventions of Morphophonemic Grammar and Usage

## With case ending

### 157

If a vowel or a consonant takes the final position in a noun where the third case marker is dropped and if it is followed by an action word beginning with a hard consonant that admits its doubling,¹⁷ then either there is no change or there appears the consonant. One ought to refer to the chapter on the study of cases.

## —the second—

### 158

Morphophonemic rules for the nouns with or without the case marker | ai |.

The occurrence of a hard consonant instead of a soft one.

The occurrence of a nasal instead of a hard consonant.

The occurrence of the appropriate consonant instead of a zero morphome.

The disappearance of a vowel where it should have occured.

The absence of an empty morph where it should have occured.

The occurrence of the case-marker along with the empty morph.

The occurrence of the appropriate consonant in the place of an empty morph.

The occurrence of the case-marker with personal class nouns.

The occurrence of the case-marker with personal class nouns that are used in the place of non-personal class.

The absence of any change in the consonant where there ought to have been a change.

Similar occurrences and changes, all of them showing the nature of these rules.
