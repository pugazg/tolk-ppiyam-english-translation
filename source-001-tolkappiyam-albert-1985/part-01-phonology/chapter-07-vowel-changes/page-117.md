# Notes

## 21

This chapter describes the morphophonemic changes when the preceding word has a final vowel. This should never be lost sight of even when the verses do not make an explicit mention of it. Along with this you should also remember the continuity or sequence in groups of verses and see which particular group talks about which vowel ending.

Remember when a grammatical rule is declared that verse very often is complete in itself. It is a declaration. This chapter is not on grammar but on usage. Therefore what word or phrase has to be supplied to make the meaning explicit in a verse is important.

## 22

This Tamil phrase which I translate as a premodifier in a verbal phrase is an interesting case. This is a premodifier to a head word in a verbal phrase. In the following verbal phrase “went to see” went is the headword and to see is the post modifier. This modifier occurs in the preposition in the equivalent Tamil phrase and so it is called here the premodifier.

Commentators without realising how neatly the original phrase shows the structure of a phrase have used a Tamil word which shows more its function. If the function is thought of we can use the word adverbial adjunct in English because this modifier goes with a verb giving it additional meaning.

## 23

As | r | helps the pronunciation of two vowels when they are said together in English and it is called the intrusive. This Tamil string of phonemes is translated an intrusive on the same analogy. It is non-semantic because the word itself carries no semantic description.

## 24

“Kind hearted man” is a noun phrase where man is the head word; kind and hearted are premodifiers. The Tamil phrase thought of here is an equivalent to this English and so in translation “premodifier in a noun phrase” is used.
