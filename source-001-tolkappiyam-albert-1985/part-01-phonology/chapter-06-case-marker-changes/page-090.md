# 6. A Chapter on the Changes in Usage With Case Markers

### 189

The same holds good with the final | m | sounds in | tām | and | nām |; the same is true of | yām | where the final | ā | in | yā | becomes | e | and the | y | there disappears. In the other two the opening long sounds are shortened.

## — | vaṟṟu |

### 190

The empty morph | vaṟṟu | occurs after | ellām | in a series with | um | at the end.

## — | nam |

### 191

| nam | occurs inbetween if the word refers to the personal class.

## — | tam |, | num |

### 192

It is said that the consonant and the | u | at the end of | ellārum | of the third person and | ellīrum | of the second person disappear; the consonant | r | and the | um | remain at the final positions, while | tam | comes inbetween these for the third person and | num | for the second person.

## —with | ṉ | endings

### 193

The final sounds of the two | tāṉ | and | yāṉ | do not behave differently from what it is in the three nouns mentioned above.

## — | attu |, | iṉ |

### 194

The learned tell us that | attu | and | iṉ | alternate with the two words | aḻaṉ | and | puḻaṉ | rightly.

## — | aṉ |

### 195

It is said to be quite natural to add the empty morph | aṉ | after | ēḻ |.
